Landlord tips

Preparing your property to let.

A well-prepared property is easier to market, more appealing to tenants and gives the tenancy the best possible start.

01

Start with the condition

Walk through the property as a tenant would. Deal with obvious damage, tired finishes and anything that could make the property feel poorly maintained.

Repairs

Fix obvious damage, leaks, loose fittings and anything that could make the property feel poorly maintained.

Decoration

Touch up tired paintwork and worn finishes. Clean, neutral presentation usually photographs and views better.

Fixtures & fittings

Check doors, windows, lights, appliances and anything supplied with the property is working properly.

02

Get ready for viewings

First impressions matter. A clean, tidy and well-presented property will photograph better and give prospective tenants more confidence when they view it.

Clean and tidy

Clean throughout, remove clutter and make each room feel bright, fresh and ready for viewings.

First impressions

Tidy the entrance, move bins out of sight and make sure any outside or communal areas look presentable.

Light and space

Open curtains and blinds, switch on lights and avoid overcrowding rooms so the property feels as spacious as possible.

03

Get the compliance in place

Before marketing, make sure the property has the required safety checks, certificates and documents in place so the tenancy can start smoothly.

Safety certificates

Make sure the required safety checks and certificates are valid, up to date and ready before the property is let.

Licensing & permissions

Check whether the property needs any licence or other permission, and make sure everything required is in place.

Documents ready

Keep the EPC, safety certificates and key tenancy paperwork organised so nothing delays the move-in.

04

Find the right tenant

Good preparation helps attract better enquiries, but choosing the right tenant matters just as much. Market clearly, carry out proper checks and set expectations from the start.

Marketing

Use clear photos, an accurate description and a sensible asking rent to attract the right level of interest.

Tenant checks

Reference applicants properly and check affordability, identity and Right to Rent before agreeing the tenancy.

Set expectations

Make sure the tenant understands the rent, property responsibilities and how the tenancy will be managed from the start.

05

Prepare for move-in

Once a tenant is agreed, make sure everything is ready for a smooth handover. Sort the final checks, keys and property information before the tenancy begins.

Final checks

Walk through the property one last time and make sure agreed repairs, cleaning and preparation work have been completed.

Keys & access

Prepare enough working keys for the tenants and make sure access arrangements are clear before move-in day.

Property information

Have important instructions, appliance information and useful property details ready for the tenant.

06

Keep everything organised

Good records make managing the tenancy much easier. Keep important documents, communication and property information together from the very beginning.

Tenancy documents

Keep the tenancy agreement, deposit information and supporting documents stored safely and easy to access.

Property records

Keep copies of certificates, inspections, inventories and maintenance records together and up to date.

Financial records

Keep clear records of rent, invoices and property costs so you always know where things stand.
