01

Preparing your property

A well-presented property gives the tenancy the best possible start. Before marketing, focus on condition, safety and the small details that make the property easier to let.

Condition

Deal with obvious repairs, damage and tired finishes before the property goes to market.

Presentation

Clean thoroughly and make each room feel bright, tidy and ready for viewings.

Readiness

Make sure keys, access, appliances and essential property information are ready before enquiries and viewings begin.

02

Setting the right rent

Look at comparable local homes and current demand to find a sensible asking rent for your property.

Market comparison

Look at similar properties nearby to understand what tenants are currently paying.

Demand

Consider how quickly comparable homes are letting and how much interest they're receiving.

Realistic pricing

Set a rent that reflects the property and market rather than simply aiming for the highest possible figure.

03

Getting the compliance right

Check the current requirements for your property and tenancy, and keep the relevant documents organised and up to date.

Safety checks

Keep the required property safety checks and certificates up to date and easy to access.

Tenancy paperwork

Make sure the tenancy, deposit and important property documents are correctly recorded and stored.

Stay up to date

Track renewal dates and changing requirements so nothing important gets missed.

04

Finding and managing tenants

A clear letting process and good communication help set expectations and support a positive tenancy.

Marketing the property

Use strong photos, a clear listing and prompt replies to attract the right level of interest.

Tenant checks

Reference applicants carefully and check affordability, identity and Right to Rent requirements.

Managing communication

Set expectations early, keep communication clear and help the tenancy start on the right foot.

05

Maintenance and ongoing management

Plan for repairs, respond promptly to issues and keep tenants informed throughout the tenancy.

Responsive repairs

Deal with maintenance issues promptly and use trusted contractors to minimise disruption.

Routine upkeep

Schedule inspections and regular maintenance so small issues don't become costly problems.

Clear communication

Keep tenants updated, confirm next steps and maintain clear records of work carried out.

06

Keeping good records

Keep tenancy paperwork, communication, maintenance notes and financial records in one accessible place.

Tenancy paperwork

Keep tenancy agreements, deposit details and key documents safely stored and easy to access.

Maintenance records

Track repairs, contractor work and property notes so you have a clear history when issues arise.

Financial records

Keep rent records, invoices and statements in one place so reporting and landlord updates stay straightforward.
